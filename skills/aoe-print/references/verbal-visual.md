## Transferable decisions

- Start with a meaningful relationship between image and words. Test whether either alone loses the central idea.
- Let one element dominate by area, silhouette, or contrast; repeated supporting units can establish rhythm without a giant title.
- Keep repeated units consistent enough to read as a family, varying position or grouping with intent. Preserve essential identifying features and legibility when overlapping.
- Make color perform a role such as distinguishing one voice or reading layer. Do not prescribe blue.
- Use actual language from the new brief. Invented generic labels weaken a concept that depends on recognition.

Do not reproduce the figure, phrases, palette, and label placements as a bundle. Apply the relationship to new content with a new visual argument. Do not add distress or corporate irony to unrelated subjects.

## Limits and checks

This is one example, not evidence that scattered labels improve all posters. The technique can become cluttered, repetitive, inaccessible at small sizes, or tonally inappropriate. Identify what must be read and at what distance. Check the dominant silhouette at thumbnail size and the labels at intended reading size. For an event poster, ensure the concept does not displace required date, place, and action information.

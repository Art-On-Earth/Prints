# Common Ground — composition transfer pilot

2026-10-05. Designer chose “Passing it on” from three concepts for a fictional neighborhood plant exchange. A full-portrait illustration was generated with a diagonal handoff, one shared pot, cropped forearms, and planned quiet areas for text. The poster was composed as a continuous field before asset generation; no rectangular image panel or footer band was added.

Real SVG typography overlays the raster asset. Gill Sans bold provides a broad title silhouette; the identifying line is semibold and the invitation regular. Event logistics were not supplied, so the concept explicitly says date and location are to be confirmed. This is a demonstration, not a real event announcement.

Browser inspection shows no clipped text, the handoff remains visible, and the information group is separate from the picture's dense area. Figma import and physical-size proof are untested. The illustration is raster; editable text is retained. The initial draft received the designer feedback recorded below; the latest revision is not yet approved. The title remains above the illustration, evaluated as coordinated separate regions rather than assuming overlap is necessary. Inspect whether that relationship is deliberate enough and whether the result is too safe or repetitive of previous pilots. No improvement claim without reviewer feedback or a baseline.

## Designer feedback
The designer liked the composition better and liked the image, but rejected the font. They noted that clean text did not relate to the paper texture, requested restrained consideration of edges/widths without harming readability, and questioned whether the title or plant was the focal point. They suggested reducing image scale and noted a preference for tall serif possibilities in nature-related contexts without prescribing serif for this revision. The revision selects the handoff as the focal action, tries a lighter upright Bodoni title with a separate subtle ink filter, and requests a smaller illustration group within the same full-page field. This is pending review; do not treat the revised type or hierarchy as accepted.

## Statement title correction
The designer found the isolated title's muted color boring and the stepped line alignment arbitrary. The next revision aligns both lines at the same left margin as the supporting copy and changes the title to a stronger terracotta red related to the pot. This acknowledges that color makes the title a stronger statement and requires reassessing its balance with the image. Awaiting review.

## Corner spacing and current inspection
The designer requested matching horizontal and vertical margins for the lower-left information block. The group was moved upward by 17 SVG units, preserving its internal hierarchy and left anchor. Browser inspection after reload shows visually comparable bottom and left insets, no clipped copy, and the continuous paper field. This is an optical check, not a measured physical proof. The current title is terracotta, left-aligned Bodoni; the illustration has been reduced from the initial draft. Final designer acceptance, native Figma import, and a printed proof remain unverified.

## Sturdier title trial
After the designer accepted the spacing but still disliked the font, the title was changed from Bodoni to Roboto Serif, condensed width 75, medium weight 500, optical size 72. The font is embedded in the preview SVG, with its OFL license beside the artifact; the title remains editable SVG text. Terracotta color, left alignment, and the restrained ink treatment remain. Browser inspection confirms the revised title renders without clipping. This is a proposed font correction awaiting designer review, not an approved font rule. Figma import remains untested.

## Rejected condensed serif
The designer explicitly rejected the Roboto Serif trial and directed further typography research through Fonts In Use. Five initial contextual studies are recorded in references/fonts-in-use.md. No replacement has been approved; the current preview still contains the rejected trial. Future selection should compare character in the actual title, rather than treating a weight/width adjustment as sufficient.

## Proportion studies
The designer rejected all three first-round candidates; DM Serif Display Italic was only the least disliked, not accepted. The next three studies deliberately vary horizontal/vertical scale and tracking on editable text, following an explicit request to experiment with distortion. These are pending review and do not replace the main poster.

# After Hours — typography transfer pilot

2026-10-05. Designer selected concept 2, “Everybody brings a chair,” from three proposed concepts for a fictional neighborhood music festival. This is a guided SVG construction, not an independent with/without-skill evaluation.

Copy: After Hours; A neighborhood music festival; Live music, food & dancing; 18 October; 17:00–23:00; Market Square; All ages welcome.

The design uses four original vector chair illustrations, a guitar, food, and a scarf around a large serif title. Supporting roles use a semibold event identifier, regular italic activities, bold date/place, and regular time. The date and place form one group. No repeated date, editorial masthead, divider, or decorative dots were added.

Browser inspection confirmed the title and illustrations dominate, attendance information forms a distinct cluster, and no visible text is clipped. The illustration is stylized vector work; the file preserves separate text and shape elements. Native Figma import remains untested. The composition is more organized and open than the dense jazz reference; assess whether it achieves enough energy and integration rather than claiming it has already passed a density test.

Pending designer review: do the objects communicate a gathering rather than furniture advertising? Are the central text groups related enough to the illustrations? Can the event type and date/place be found easily? Is the visual rhythm too symmetrical or safe? No general effectiveness or user acceptance is claimed yet.

## Designer review — rejected
The designer rejected the first result: illustrations were plain rather than meaningfully minimal, the title/event-description/activity hierarchy was confusing, and the fonts were unsuitable. They requested grain as an available skill capability. Record this as a failed transfer test, not a successful application of the Open Draft lesson.

Next revision criteria: test one illustration for expressive character before multiplying it; compare actual title typography; consolidate the secondary message into a legible reading unit; establish clear attendance information; and compare clean/texture variants only after the underlying composition works. Grain is a requested capability, not evidence that applying noise will solve this result. No revised artifact has yet been reviewed.

## Second illustration and poster
The illustration was rebuilt using image generation as a separate text-free raster asset: overlapping chairs, drawn contours, dry-brush grain, guitar, food, and scarf. The designer called the illustration “much better” before seeing the complete poster. This approves the illustration direction only, not the typography or finished layout.

The revised SVG places that asset beneath a DIN Condensed title and one grouped event-description block, with attendance details in a contrasting footer. DIN Condensed, Rockwell, and Futura title specimens were rendered locally before choosing the condensed treatment. Text remains SVG text; illustration is raster and its grain is baked into that asset, not independently adjustable. Figma import, font substitution, print production, and full-poster acceptance remain untested.

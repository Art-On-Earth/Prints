# Three concepts before execution

For a new brief without an agreed direction, show three short concepts, then wait for the designer's choice. Do not build three finished posters to answer a concept request. If the designer has delegated the choice, recommend and develop one without an artificial approval gate.

Each concept takes two or three sentences:
- **Idea:** the relationship that makes this message memorable, grounded in its content.
- **Composition:** the first thing seen, the next thing read, and how type and imagery relate.
- **Fit and tradeoff:** why this works for the audience, and what it sacrifices.

Describe specific decisions instead of mood labels such as “bold modern editorial.” Distinguish the three through their ideas and reading structures, not just visual treatments. An object metaphor, a typographic interpretation of the language, and a narrative sequence can differ meaningfully, but do not force these same three categories on every brief.

Recommend one by communication fit, not novelty alone. Mention only assumptions that materially affect selection. Avoid inventing copy, assets, or an event to make an idea sound complete.

## Taste with foundations

Ask what the design says before asking what it looks like. Establish the relationship between message, dominant element, secondary reading, and quiet information. A title need not be the biggest element; a poster need not have a conventional title at all when the supplied content communicates through another structure.

Treat “does not look like AI” as a demand for specificity and judgment, not a recognizable anti-AI style. Critique empty decoration, stock metaphors, unexplained hierarchy, indiscriminate textures, and recycled layouts by their effect on this brief. Gradients, symmetry, polished imagery, and familiar type are not inherently wrong. Roughness does not establish originality.

Playfulness should have a mechanism: contradiction, timing, scale, repetition, ambiguity, or a meaningful unexpected connection. Do not add whimsy to a solemn brief. Any broken convention must preserve the job's necessary reading and use.
